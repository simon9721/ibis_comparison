Eye diagram notes for the clean PRBS7 + RLGC rerun

The first generated eye folders, eyes_ngspice and eyes_xyce, used --auto_phase
to align the fold start directly to the receiver threshold crossings. That is
not a good review convention: it makes the plots look like repeated transition
or step responses.

Corrected review folders:

1. eyes_ngspice_1ui_decision
2. eyes_xyce_1ui_decision

These use a 1-UI window, crossing-to-crossing, with the decision point centered
on the x-axis:

  --n_ui 1 --auto_phase --auto_phase_target crossing --center_x

Also generated:

1. eyes_ngspice_clock_centered
2. eyes_xyce_clock_centered

These use a 2-UI window with the decision point at the plot center:

  --n_ui 2 --auto_phase --auto_phase_target decision --center_x

Important correction after review:

The clock-folded eyes still do not force rising and falling edges to intersect
at both UI boundaries. They preserve the raw receiver timing, including the
measured rise/fall crossing phase split:

  ngspice V(n10b) rising crossing phase ~= 0.608 UI
  ngspice V(n10b) falling crossing phase ~= 0.334 UI

That duty-cycle distortion is why one side can look mostly rising while the
other side looks mostly falling.

Later review correction:

The edge-aligned folders below are diagnostic transition overlays only. They
should not be used as the physically correct eye result, because they detect
receiver threshold crossings and redraw each transition family at both UI
boundaries. That intentionally removes the rise/fall phase split from the
transition locations.

1. eyes_ngspice_simple_edge_with_rails
2. eyes_xyce_simple_edge_with_rails

They used:

  --fold_mode edge --edge_include_clock_rails

The eye tool has since been reverted to clock/UI-grid folding only. That means
the eye plot should preserve the transient waveform's duty-cycle distortion
instead of compensating it visually.
