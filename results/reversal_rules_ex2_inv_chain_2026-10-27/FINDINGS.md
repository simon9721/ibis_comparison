# The two simplest reversal rules on ex2 and inv_chain

*2026-10-27.* `scripts/reversal_rules_ex2_inv_chain.py`; the cases run through
`scripts/archive/compare_silicon_anchored_shortpulse.py` with the pybis gate modes of the
2026-08-20 stress matrix; the reversal-step analysis through a gz-aware copy of
`scripts/archive/analyse_reversal_discontinuity.py` (`jumps/`).

## Question

When a short pulse reverses, what happens if the model simply re-enters the opposite coefficient
table, either at the same elapsed time (**time matching**) or where that table holds the present
coefficient value (**value matching**, the `coeff_match` build gated to the reversal)? Both were
run in the 2026-08-20 matrix, but the matrix scored a whole-record pad rms, which a model that
produces no pulse at all can win, and time matching had no number there because it never
simulated. Here both are run on the two base buffers the paper follows, at the five widths of
its Table X, and scored as the paper scores: the peak error of the pad pulse against the
transistor, (28), and the pad rms over the pulse and its return, the window of (29).

**Time matching did not simulate on any of the ten cases.** ngspice's timestep collapses 26 to
30 ps after the reverse edge at the re-entry node (`xdrv.hfall_after_rise` or
`xdrv.hreverse_edge`): the rule demands a step in the coefficient of 0.48 to 1.83 on ex2 and
2.00 on inv_chain (`jumps/jumps.csv`, |dK_u| + |dK_d|), against 0.00 to 0.21 for value matching
and 0.06 to 0.50 for a gate-state re-entry. The step is the discontinuity the solver cannot pass.
This is the same outcome as in the matrix (ten of ten failures per buffer there too).

**Value matching produces no pulse.** On every ex2 case the model's K_u never exceeds 0.03 and
its pad stays within 2 mV of its rail: peak error −100 %. On inv_chain K_u reaches 0.07 to 0.48
but the pad peak is 1 to 12 % of the transistor's. The matrix's pad rms of 90 to 206 mV for
this method on ex2 was the whole-record average of a flat model against a pulse of under 1 ns in
a 22 ns record; over the pulse itself the rms is 300 to 650 mV, worse than native on seven of the
ten cases. The waveforms are identical to the matrix's for the same cases (same mode, same
model, same references), so this is a re-scoring with the paper's metric, not a new behaviour.

Why it fails: at the reversal the present K_u is partway up the rising curve. The falling table
holds that value only near its own end, after the pull-up has mostly turned off, so the rule
enters the falling table at a point where the coefficient is about to reach zero, and the pulse
is cut off. Value matching matches the number and not the state; the same K_u belongs to a
device that is turning on and to one that has nearly turned off.

## What this says for the paper

Native IBIS replays the complete transition and overshoots (+71 % on ex2 at 810 ps). The two
simplest alternatives to replay do worse, in opposite ways: keeping the clock cannot be
simulated because of the step it demands, and matching the coefficient value extinguishes the
pulse because a coefficient value is not a state. The model needs a state variable that the
reversal can interrupt, which is the gate of the chain. These numbers belong in the related-work
or motivation paragraph of the paper, scored on the peak as Table X is.
